The central decision

Why summarise-then-search cannot work

The obvious design is to summarise the video, then search the summary. It fails for a reason worth stating precisely.

A summary of twenty-four hours of hallway footage will never contain father took his medicine at 08:47 unless something already forced a model to look at that moment and write it down. Summarisation is lossy by construction, so a search over summaries is a search over a document that has already discarded the thing being looked for.

The working shape is the opposite. Build a dense, timestamped, multi-modal index over the footage; let the query search that; then have a model verify the top candidates before answering. Summaries become a by-product — a daily digest worth having — rather than the search substrate.

Expectations

Three questions, three different problems

These read as one feature and are not. The split was called before any code was written; what follows is what each tier actually does now.

When did my son go outside?
Built Person identity plus a zone crossing. Verified end to end: enrolling one sighting names the same person elsewhere at 0.998 similarity and refuses a stranger at 0.034. Returns exact timestamps.
When did father take his medicine?
Not built Fine-grained action on a small object. Needs a vision-language model captioning person crops, which nothing here does. The one genuinely missing capability.
Who broke the glasses?
Research-grade Causal attribution over a state change. The honest version surfaces everyone present in the window and lets a person decide. Unchanged from the original assessment.

Architecture

Cheap stages gate expensive ones

CCTV is 90–97% static. The entire performance argument is that nothing expensive should ever see a frame a cheaper filter could have discarded. Each stage runs only on what the previous one passed.

The cheapest stage

Stage 0 is itself two tiers

Tier A triages from compressed packet sizes without decoding anything. In an inter-frame codec a P-frame encodes only what changed, so packet size tracks motion, and demuxing is bounded by disk speed rather than CPU. Tier B then decodes only what Tier A passed, at three frames a second and 320 pixels wide, and scores it with MOG2 background subtraction.

Tier A is deliberately tuned for recall over precision: a false candidate costs a little decode time, whereas a miss is unrecoverable, because Tier B never sees what Tier A drops. That asymmetry is why the third path below exists.

What actually runs

Five small vision models, and where each one runs

None of these are runtime dependencies. torch, ultralytics, transformers and insightface are used once, from a throwaway environment, purely to produce ONNX files. At inference the app is ONNX Runtime, numpy and CTranslate2.

YOLO11n10.7 MBFind objects — people, vehicles, animals, bagsIntel iGPU

These are the defaults, and two of them cannot be shipped: YOLO11 is AGPL-3.0 and InsightFace's SCRFD and ArcFace are released for research only. A permissively licensed alternative now exists for each — YOLOX-tiny under Apache-2.0, and YuNet and SFace under MIT and Apache-2.0 — reachable through tsv setup --detector yolox-tiny and the permissive face configuration. They are a deliberate opt-in rather than a swap, because the cosine thresholds differ between face stacks and a silent change would invalidate every identity already enrolled.

The best backend is a property of the model, not the machine

Measured per forward pass on the baseline laptop — an i5-1235U with Intel UHD graphics and no discrete GPU:

ModelIntel iGPUCPUWinner
YOLO11n @ 64033 ms45 msiGPU
SCRFD @ 64054 ms24 msCPU
ArcFace @ 11224 ms9 msCPU
CLIP image @ 22482 ms51 msCPU
CLIP text @ 77 tok34 ms36 msa wash

It is not about model size — CLIP's image encoder is by far the largest graph here and still loses on the iGPU. Only a sustained, convolution-heavy workload keeps that GPU busy enough to pay back the cost of moving data to it. Compile time compounds it: the iGPU takes four to six seconds to compile a graph the CPU loads in half a second, which a user feels on their first search. So the detector runs on the iGPU while the face and CLIP stacks run on the CPU, in the same pass.

The question everyone asks

There is no LLM in the answer path

Nothing generates text when a question is answered. A vision-language model does run during indexing, to caption what a tracked person is doing — but by the time you type, its output is just more words in the index.

Question understanding is entity grounding, not language understanding, and that is what lets it work with no model at all. The vocabulary a question can draw on is closed and already in the database: the people who were enrolled, the zones that were drawn, the classes the detector knows, the cameras that exist, and dates. Matching against that is reliable in a way that parsing arbitrary English is not, and it costs nothing to run.

Whatever survives after the known entities are lifted out becomes the CLIP query, which handles the genuinely open-vocabulary half — "in a red jacket", "carrying a box". Everything the index knows exactly is answered exactly; only the fuzzy remainder is left to a similarity score.

The limitation is honest. The direction phrases — went out, came home — are a fixed list, not an understanding of English, and real use will throw up phrasings that need adding.

Both of the pieces this section once listed as missing are now built, and neither added a torch runtime dependency: Florence-2-base captions person crops through ONNX Runtime, which is what makes the medicine class of question answerable at all, and Whisper reads speech through CTranslate2. The cost warning stands — on a CPU-only machine, captioning a day of footage is an overnight batch rather than something to run while waiting.

Hard-won

What testing found that reading would not

Each of these was silent. The code ran, produced plausible output, and was wrong in a way no exception would ever surface.

GOP SAWTOOTH

A P-frame's cost depends on its position in the GOP

Frames just after a keyframe have little residual left to code, so with a fixed GOP the raw sizes carry a periodic swing larger than a walking person. Each packet is now scored against the median size for its own position in the GOP.

Idle-floor variation before: 11.8%  →  after: 3.4%

LOCAL ALL-INTRA

Encoders stop emitting P-frames when a scene gets noisy

Heavy IR noise trips scene-cut detection on every frame and the encoder falls back to all keyframes. A whole-file check misses it: the global ratio stays low while half the recording is silently unreadable.

After an IR flip: 600 of 600 frames were keyframes, while the file-wide ratio read 0.586 — under the threshold, so its entire second half scored zero.

MEDIAN BASELINE

Motion only ever pushes bins upward

The idle floor was taken as the median, which is only correct when activity is a minority. On a short file where someone is on screen half the time, the median sits inside the activity and hides exactly what raised it. Plenty of NVRs write one-minute files, so that case is normal rather than exotic. It is now a low quantile.

SILENT EXPORT

The CLIP graph returned the wrong tensor

get_image_features returns a tuple in transformers 5.x, so the ONNX export emitted two outputs and the first was raw per-token hidden state, not the projection. Vectors came out 38400 long instead of 512. Nothing crashed; search would simply have been meaningless.

ALIGNMENT

A face crop is not a face

ArcFace is trained on faces warped so the eyes, nose and mouth corners sit at fixed positions. A plain box crop produces embeddings that look plausible and match the wrong people.

Same face, aligned against itself: 0.99. From a plain box crop: 0.68. Two different people: 0.00.

CONFIDENTLY WRONG

Answering about someone who was never enrolled

Asking about a person the index had never heard of left their name in the fuzzy remainder while the zone and direction still matched — so it answered with a confident set of timestamps belonging to a different person. A caveat underneath was not enough. A capitalised leftover is now a refusal.

CONCURRENCY

One SQLite handle across a threadpool

check_same_thread=False silences the ownership check but does not make a connection safe. A page requesting several thumbnails at once raised bad parameter or other API misuse. Latent since the first phase; the search page simply made the browser concurrent enough to expose it.

INVISIBLE OVERLAY

A hidden element that was not hidden

The video player's backdrop is position: fixed with display: flex, and that declaration overrides its own hidden attribute — the rule implementing [hidden] is a user-agent rule, which loses to any author rule. The overlay covered the whole window from page load and swallowed every click.

elementFromPoint at the centre of the app returned video. Missed originally by checking the hidden property rather than what rendered.

The product

A window you drop a video on

The pipeline works from a command line, but that is not a product. There is now a native window: drop a video, watch it being read, type what you are looking for, click a frame to play from just before that moment. If nothing matches it says so rather than handing back the closest three frames in the library.

It is the same local server in a WebView2 frame rather than a second implementation, so the window, a browser and the planned Android client all talk to the same endpoints. What the window adds is a native file dialog returning real paths, so a video is indexed where it already lives rather than being uploaded to the machine it is already on.

Importing never blocks the app. Motion segmentation finishes early and its moments are searchable immediately, so search goes live while analysis is still running — verified typing at 15% through an import. Progress is measured in seconds of footage examined rather than files or segments, because a long recording often yields two segments and a bar that moves twice in four minutes is indistinguishable from a hung one.

One reversal worth recording. The library has no default similarity floor, because absolute CLIP scores are not calibrated and a caller that can see them should choose its own. The app applies one anyway: its whole promise is an answer or an honest nothing, and returning the closest three frames for "a snowy mountain" reads as broken. The number lives in config, is shown as a percentage on every result, and is one line to change.

What is not calibrated

Every threshold is a guess. Synthetic clips prove correctness, not calibration, and the difference matters. In rough order of how likely each is to bite:

  • Night and IR scenes. A dark subject on a dark IR background scored roughly ten times lower than the same subject in daylight — close enough to the segment-open threshold to matter.
  • Noisy cameras weaken Tier A. The packet signal is relative, so sensor noise shrinks it. On a deliberately noisy clip the gap between idle and active fell to about three sigma with the two populations touching — no threshold could have separated them.
  • Identity thresholds. Separation on clean frontal faces is enormous, 0.998 against 0.034. CCTV faces are small, angled and often IR-lit, and that margin will shrink.
  • Search relevance. A matching query scored 0.22–0.27 and an unrelated one 0.15, but that gap moves with the camera and the phrasing. "A helicopter landing" still scores 0.22 against a person walking.
  • Recording timestamps. Filenames are read before container metadata, because NVRs rewrite creation_time on export. Where neither exists the fallback is file mtime, recorded per video so it can be distrusted.
  • Question phrasing. The direction phrases are a list, not an understanding of English.

The gating item has not changed since the first day of this work: real footage. A few days of actual recordings including night, IR and some weather. Every number above was measured on synthetic clips or on a single phone video, and the first honest calibration pass needs cameras pointed at a real place.
